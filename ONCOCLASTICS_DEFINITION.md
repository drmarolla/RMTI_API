# Oncoclastics: definition note (version 1.0.0)

**Oncoclastics** (adjective: *oncoclastic*; from *onco-*, tumor, and *-clastic*, broken or fragmented, from the Greek *klastos*) is the application of hazard–exposure–vulnerability–resilience assessment, as developed in disaster-risk reduction, to the fragmentation of cancer-related trajectories, whether biological (the progression from chronic injury to cancer; *biological oncoclastics*) or systemic (the disruption of surveillance and care; *care-system oncoclastics*).

To our knowledge the term is new. An oncolytic agent destroys tumor cells (Russell et al., 2012) and an osteoclast resorbs bone (Teitelbaum, 2000); what is broken here is the continuity of a trajectory or care pathway, not the tumor.

The biological sense (the path from chronic liver injury toward HCC, including noncirrhotic HCC) is the trajectory of interest; the care-system sense enters only through the care-system component of ρ: ρ = 1 − (1 − ρ_bio)(1 − ρ_care). The same framing applies to oncology in general, including lung and breast cancer.

| Term | Biological oncoclastics | Care-system oncoclastics (through ρ) |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | Individual patient; progression from chronic injury toward HCC | Care pathway of the same patient |
| L | Probability of progression to cancer over the horizon | Not changed by disruption of care |
| E | Burden of metabolic, inflammatory, toxic or hemodynamic insults | Not changed by disruption of care |
| V | Allostatic load carried forward across cycles | Not scored separately |
| ρ | Biological adaptive reserve, ρ_bio | Care-system capacity, ρ_care |

The analogy to disaster risk is structural, not literal. Scores are relative and uncalibrated; no clinical action is attached to any tier. Research prototype, not a medical device.

Attribution: the Risk Mechanism Theory Index (RMTI), including its adaptation to oncology, was developed by Marolla (2018, 2026a, 2026b). In every use RMTI denotes the Risk Mechanism Theory Index; give the full name at first use.
